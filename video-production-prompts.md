# CrisisLink Video Production Prompts

Use these prompts step-by-step to produce the CrisisLink pitch video.

---

## STEP 1 — Voiceover (ElevenLabs)

Go to https://elevenlabs.io → Projects → Create → paste each block below as a separate segment.
Use voice **"Adam"** (authoritative) or **"Rachel"** (warm/clear).

---

### Narrator Block 1 — Cold Open

Her name doesn't matter right now. What matters is that she's real. She's sitting in that waiting room tonight. And tomorrow night, if something doesn't change — she might be sitting on a sidewalk.

We live in the wealthiest nation in human history. We have more hospitals, more shelters, more social programs, more funding, more technology than any civilization that has ever existed. And yet — people are falling through the cracks. Not because the resources aren't there. But because no one can find them in time.

---

### Narrator Block 2 — The System Is Broken

Meet the American social safety net. It is, by almost every measure, an administrative labyrinth. A person in crisis — someone who has just lost their housing, their job, their safety — must navigate a maze of phone numbers, intake forms, eligibility requirements, waitlists, and referral letters that can take days, weeks, or months to process.

The average person seeking emergency shelter makes contact with 4.3 different agencies before finding a bed — if they find one at all. The average referral from a case worker to a housing program takes 3 to 5 business days to process. For someone sleeping outside in January, that is not a delay. That is a death sentence.

This isn't a failure of compassion. The people working in this system — the case workers, the social workers, the shelter staff — they are working themselves to exhaustion. This is a failure of infrastructure. A failure of connection. And it is costing lives.

---

### Narrator Block 3 — The Data

Let's talk numbers.

Over half a million people. On one night. That we counted.

Forty-four million people don't know where their next meal is coming from.

The resources to address these crises exist. The funding exists. The programs exist. What doesn't exist — is the bridge between the person in need and the help they deserve.

---

### Narrator Block 4 — Bias and Gatekeeping

There is something else inside this broken system. Something we don't like to talk about. Something that data confirms, that audits reveal, that people who have lived through it carry like a scar.

Bias.

Black Americans are disproportionately denied referrals to transitional housing. LGBTQ youth are turned away from faith-based shelters at nearly three times the rate of their peers. People with mental illness are flagged as high-risk and deprioritized — the very people who need the most help, told to wait.

When a human being is the gatekeeper, human bias enters the door. It is not always malicious. It is often unconscious. But the impact is the same. People who need help — don't get it.

What if we could remove the gatekeeper entirely?

---

### Narrator Block 5 — Intermission

Every system we are about to show you — we built it because of her. Because of him. Because of the 582,000 people sleeping outside tonight. Because the people working in this system deserve better tools. Because the people funding this work deserve to see results. And because the people in crisis deserve — at minimum — a fair chance.

---

### Narrator Block 6 — Introducing CrisisLink

CrisisLink.

Not a hotline. Not a website with a list of phone numbers. Not another form to fill out and wait on. CrisisLink is an AI-powered platform that does what the current system cannot — it connects a person in crisis directly to the resources they need, applies on their behalf, and creates a complete, auditable record of every step.

Here's how it works. A person in crisis opens CrisisLink. They answer a few simple questions — or speak them aloud. Within seconds, the AI has assessed their situation: their location, their immediate needs, their eligibility for programs. It doesn't judge. It doesn't profile. It processes.

Then it acts. Automatically. Applications sent. Referrals delivered. Not tomorrow. Not next week. Now. While the person is still standing in the waiting room. While they still have battery on their phone.

No phone trees. No callbacks. No gatekeepers. Just — help.

---

### Narrator Block 7 — The Case Worker's Tool

CrisisLink doesn't replace case workers. It gives them superpowers.

Every client who uses CrisisLink has a complete, portable chart. When a case worker logs in, they see everything — every agency the client has contacted, every application submitted, every outcome. No more starting from scratch. No more duplicated intake forms. No more lost records when a client transfers between counties.

Built-in video calling means a case worker can meet with a client anywhere — without the client needing transportation, without a waiting room, without an appointment that takes three weeks to schedule.

---

### Narrator Block 8 — The Funder's View

For decades, funders — government agencies, foundations, corporate sponsors — have poured money into social services and asked a simple question: Is it working?

The answer, almost always, has been: We think so. Here's a report.

CrisisLink gives funders something they've never had before: transparent, real-time proof of impact. How many people were helped today. Which programs worked. Which ones didn't. Where the gaps are. Where the money made the biggest difference.

Every dollar, accountable. Every outcome, measurable. Every person served, counted — with full privacy protections and ethical data governance.

No more guessing. No more faith-based funding. Evidence-based investment — in the people who need it most.

---

### Narrator Block 9 — The Auditable Trail

When something goes wrong in the current system — and things do go wrong — there is rarely a clear record of what happened. Who made what decision. When. Why. A case falls through the cracks and nobody can trace the crack.

CrisisLink maintains a complete, tamper-resistant audit trail of every action taken on every case. For accountability. For legal compliance. For the protection of the people being served — and the professionals serving them.

If a decision is made, it's recorded. If a referral is denied, the reason is logged. If a bed was available and wasn't offered — that is visible.

Transparency isn't just good ethics. It's the only way to build trust with the public, with government, and with the communities this system is supposed to serve.

---

### Narrator Block 10 — The Human Moment

She got a bed that night. Not because the system suddenly worked perfectly. Because a tool was in place that found her a bed in 11 minutes. Because someone built a bridge between where she was and what she needed.

That's what this is. Not a technology project. A rescue operation — built for scale.

---

### Narrator Block 11 — The Ask

We are proposing a phased rollout — beginning in two pilot cities, measuring outcomes rigorously, and expanding based on evidence. This is not a moonshot. This is a blueprint.

Building CrisisLink requires three things.

First — technology investment. Building and maintaining the AI platform, the mobile applications, the data infrastructure, and the security systems that protect the people who use it.

Second — government partnership. CrisisLink cannot function without integration into existing systems — shelter databases, healthcare referral networks, county case management platforms. We need regulatory approval. We need data-sharing agreements. We need government not as a barrier, but as a collaborator.

Third — institutional trust. From the organizations on the ground. From the case workers. From the communities. That trust is earned by building with them — not for them.

---

### Narrator Block 12 — Closing

Every system that seems impossible was, at one point, just an idea. The interstate highway system. The internet. Medicare. Medicaid. Someone stood up and said: the way we're doing this isn't good enough. We can do better. And they were right.

We are not asking you to fix poverty. We are not asking you to solve homelessness with an app. We are asking for one thing:

Don't let the gap between a person and their help be caused by paperwork.

Cut the middle man. Connect the person. Change the outcome.

## STEP 3 — Assemble the Video (CapCut)

Go to https://capcut.com → New Project → open the AI assistant and paste this:

I have a 17-minute documentary video to assemble. I have AI-generated video clips and a voiceover audio track. Please help me: 1. Lay the voiceover audio as the base timeline track. 2. Cut the video clips to match the pacing of the narration. 3. Add subtle cinematic color grading — cool and dark tones for the first half (problem sections), warm tones for the second half (solution sections). 4. Add slow cross-dissolve transitions between clips (0.5 seconds). 5. Add a background music track that is dramatic and orchestral for the first half, then shifts to warm and hopeful piano and strings for the second half. 6. Fade to black at the end over 3 seconds.
